01 THE IDEA

Bias can hide
between the lines.

Choosing an answer and completing a story are different tasks. They can reveal different biases.

Multiple-choice questions make social bias easy to quantify. But they may miss the stereotypes that emerge when a language model assigns roles or decides what happens next.

SCENE brings the evaluation into the narrative. Two complementary tasks retain structured outputs, making bias directly measurable without an auxiliary LLM evaluator.

Read the abstract+

Most existing benchmarks evaluate social bias in large language models (LLMs) using multiple-choice questions (MCQ) grounded in stereotypical contexts. While MCQ formats enable straightforward quantification, how LLMs manifest social bias in task-specific contexts and under different output constraints remains underexplored. We introduce SCENE, a benchmark for measuring the social bias shown by LLMs in scenario-completion tasks. Given short scenarios involving social groups, models must complete key elements of the situation through two tasks: (i) Character Specification, which asks models to assign social attributes to characters, and (ii) Plot Continuation, which asks models to select the most plausible narrative continuation. Combining templates adapted from prior benchmarks with newly collected templates through a university-wide competition, we construct a dataset of over 75,000 Korean instances. Experiments show that bias measurements are highly sensitive to task context and output constraints, even for identical underlying scenarios, suggesting that widely used MCQ-based evaluations may not fully capture certain manifestations of stereotypes.

Q3 / TEMPLATE SOURCE

New contexts.
Newly visible biases.

Competition-derived scenarios generally elicit higher biased rates and lower neutral rates than KoBBQ-derived scenarios in Character Specification.

These contemporary templates surface underrepresented stereotypes, including occupation, personality, and technology.

The source effect is strongest in Character Specification. Plot Continuation shows smaller differences.

WORDS MATTER, TOO

Among the augmentation stages, entity refinement has the largest effect on Character Specification—highlighting the importance of diverse expressions for social groups.

THE TAKEAWAY

To understand bias,
look at the whole scene.

01

Evaluate across task framings and output constraints.

02

Include contemporary contexts and underrepresented stereotypes.

03

Keep benchmarks diverse and continuously updated.

SCENE evaluates structured scenario completion in Korean. Its results should be interpreted within these tasks and linguistic contexts.
